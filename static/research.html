BACKGROUND SOURCE REVIEW

The slides and user clarifications define the strategy.

The user designated two of their source slides as the strategy’s source of truth: DCA buys including dips, VA sells during spikes, and capture of long-term growth. The slides exclude technical indicators, influencer opinions, and news-driven investment decisions. The user separately confirmed automatic full exits and clarified that trading can continue after an exit. The slides do not specify a capture-percentage formula. Read the active requirements.

The public-source investigation below is historical context. It does not override the slides or the user’s clarifications. We followed the website of a small investment adviser that promotes a similar approach, its blog articles, linked social accounts, and public posts. The firm is not named in this project. The complete investigation is in the research report.

The closest published example

A 2026 article by the firm’s founder illustrates SPXL with $100,000, daily opening-price adjustments, DCA 2%, VA 1%, and capture 5%. The figures are illustrative; its hypothetical drawdown is 46.9%. It encourages looking at groups of parameter settings rather than picking a single historical winner. These values are not this project’s recommended settings.

A technology article on the firm’s blog describes a three-dimensional parameter space: a daily purchase amount, daily VA objective, and overall exit objective, with color representing historical profitability. The user independently confirmed that automatic full exits belong in this project. The article does not establish an exact exit-threshold formula in the slides.

Where definitions remain incomplete

Public descriptions refer variously to a daily growth objective and a margin above average entry price. They do not provide a complete recurrence, denominator, or transaction-order specification. The firm’s FAQ distinguishes public principles from proprietary analysis and execution.

Our VA growth-path formula, whole-portfolio capture threshold, and prior-open decision timing are explicit implementation choices. The active strategy uses DCA for purchases, VA for partial sales, and automatic full exits as confirmed by the user. A completed full exit can be followed by a DCA purchase in the same session, using actual net exit cash. Immediate reentry at the modeled opening is a simulation convention; the user did not prescribe an exact reentry time. Daily-open observations limit timing resolution but do not impose a strategy rule against further trading after an exit. A source’s use of leveraged ETFs does not require using them here; this prototype offers a fixed universe of unleveraged index ETFs. See the exact implemented rules.

Social accounts and operating examples

The firm’s website links its social accounts. Its public company feed contains recurring BUY, SELL, and HOLD reports and examples of full captures by individual investing allocations. That supports distinguishing partial sales from full exits, but does not establish that every account sells every holding together. The numerical results are company reports, not independent verification. Personal profiles, video transcripts, and several platforms’ post histories could not be read during this review.

Other articles

  • A history article gives a 30% example capture goal, showing there is no single universal public setting.
  • An income article shows why withdrawals, sale proceeds, and profit need separate accounting.
  • A co-founder’s engineering history describes the parameter visualization.
  • An officially linked video about index investing was not transcribed in this review.

What we did not infer

The “perfect track record” and return claims in the earlier reference material are source claims, not software guarantees. The screenshot’s assertions about MetaTrader, penny-stock income, and fundamentals forecasting are not independently established here and are not project instructions. The AWS architecture in the slides is an example; this project uses local FLOSS tools.

Some blog pages could be read only through indexed text while direct requests returned 404. Social-platform access was incomplete. No private portal or unpublished algorithm was accessed, and nobody was contacted. The linked report records these limits. Names, links, and other details that would identify the firm or the people involved are deliberately left out.
