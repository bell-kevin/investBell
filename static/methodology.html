FROM THE USER’S SLIDES AND CLARIFICATIONS

Buy steadily. Trim spikes. Capture growth.

Two of the user’s source slides define the strategy’s principles. The user also explicitly retained automatic full exits and clarified that an exit can occur before trading is finished for the day. The equations below are project choices; the slides do not specify them, and historical results are not forecasts.

What the slides require

  1. DCA to buy, including dips. Scheduled purchases continue through rises and declines when cash is available.
  2. VA to sell during spikes. Partial sales follow a documented value target, using observed prices and position accounting.
  3. Capture the long-term growth. Measure the outcome of accumulation and sales, including cash and the position still held. The slide does not supply a capture percentage; automatic full exits are confirmed separately by the user.

The second slide excludes technical indicators, influencer opinions, and news from investment decisions. It centers the approach on “indexes grow over time” and figuring out how to capture that growth. This is the strategy’s premise, not a promise about future returns. The chart’s “position average” line does not establish a mathematical VA formula.

The user clarified that DCA helps determine buys, VA helps determine sells, and exits can happen before the day’s trading is finished. DCA can therefore continue after either a VA trim or a full exit. The simulator has no mandatory wait until the next day after liquidation.

The three strategy variables

The user’s “fundamentals” mean only DCA, DVA, and capture. DVA is dollar-value averaging: the same concept labeled VA in the slides and stored as va_pct in existing files. No earnings, valuation ratios, technical indicators, or additional predictive features are introduced. Starting capital, costs, and paper execution limits are separate accounting and execution settings.

Each experiment holds one approved index ETF and cash. A cycle starts with the portfolio in cash. There are no external deposits, withdrawals, borrowing, technical indicators, news signals, or price predictions.

InputMeaning in this implementation
DCA % / dayA fixed purchase budget for each trading session, calculated as a percentage of total capital when a cycle starts. Actual spending is capped by available cash after costs.
VA % / dayDaily compounded growth of a position-value target. Each actual purchase adds its notional to that target. Excess position value schedules a partial sale.
Capture %Portfolio gain relative to cycle-start value. Meeting the threshold schedules a full exit. Its actual net cash starts the next cycle, with DCA buying allowed in that same session.

The slides do not set these percentages, the daily cadence, the percentage denominators, the VA recurrence, or a precise definition of a spike. These are inspectable modeling assumptions implementing the slide principles and the user’s full-exit clarification.

The calculation

B = total cash at cycle start
d = DCA percentage / 100
v = daily VA percentage / 100
c = capture percentage / 100

Daily DCA budget = B × d

At each session's open:
    grow the previous VA target by (1 + v)
    execute any sale planned at the previous observed open,
      unless the no-loss rule holds it (below)
    if that sale completes a full exit:
        B = actual resulting cash after sale costs
        VA target = 0
    buy B × d, capped by available cash after costs
    add the actual purchase notional to the VA target

After these trades:
    position value = shares × observed opening price
    portfolio equity = cash + position value
    if portfolio equity ≥ B × (1 + c):
        schedule ALL shares for sale at the NEXT session's open
    otherwise:
        excess = max(0, position value − VA target)
        schedule excess / observed open shares for sale at the NEXT open

A partial VA sale leaves the target intact. A full exit resets it. Sales execute before DCA purchases, so either kind of sale can be followed by a purchase in the same session. Capture takes priority over a partial trim when both conditions hold. The VA target records the chosen growth path and cumulative purchases; it is not an estimated future price or the chart’s position-average line.

For example, if a full exit leaves $12,000 in cash and DCA is 2%, the same session’s new purchase budget is $240, plus the configured fee. The remaining cash stays available. That $240 daily budget remains fixed until the next full exit.

The no-loss rule

On by default, and switchable in the form. The cost of the shares held is the cash paid for them, including buy fees. A planned VA trim or full exit is held, not sold, if its proceeds after the sale fee would be less than the cost of the shares being sold. The position stays open and the next day’s plan is recomputed as usual. This matches the stated rule, from a firm that promotes this approach, of never selling below the average entry price, and it only matters when a price gaps down between the signal and the fill, or when fees exceed a small gain.

The rule does not prevent losses. It leaves them unsold. A run can show zero losing sales while its position sits far below cost, which is how a record of closed trades can look perfect while the account is down. The lab therefore reports the worst unsold loss (the largest drop of the position below its cost) and the drawdown next to the count of held sales. Held sales appear as HOLD rows in the trade ledger.

Opening prices and execution timing

The user’s notes explicitly require opening prices, all-cash liquidation, and percentage-based reentry. The morning-after example is compatible with the earlier clarification allowing same-session continuation. Exact order timing remains an implementation choice.

A recorded opening print is observable only once trading opens. The engine uses session t’s observed open to form a sale plan, then fills at session t+1’s open with slippage. It never uses closing prices. Scheduled purchases depend on the cycle budget and cash available at the fill; a purchase after a full exit uses the new net cash balance.

The daily-open simulator represents the exit and subsequent purchase in order at the same modeled session opening. That immediate reentry is a simulation choice permitted by the user’s clarification; the user did not prescribe an exact reentry time. It cannot reproduce the timestamps or prices of arbitrary intraday trades. That resolution limit is not a strategy rule requiring trading to end after an exit. The separate Alpaca paper runner uses reconciled broker fills and later price-bounded orders; it does not assume an exact opening-price reentry. It remains an experimental execution variant with no real-money mode.

The signal delay differs from a model that inspects an opening print and assumes a conditional sale filled at that same price. An opening gap can make a queued VA sale or full exit realize a loss; “sell during spikes” describes the observed trigger, not a guaranteed execution price.

What the cube measures

The three axes are daily DCA allocation, daily VA target growth, and cycle capture threshold. Each cube represents one simulation over the same observations, starting capital, and trading costs. By default, color compares each cube with simple DCA at the same daily DCA budget, dates and costs, with no VA trims or full exits: blue finished behind, white finished within ±1%, and orange finished ahead. Deeper shades mark gaps of 5% and 20% or more. This shows what VA and capture added or lost. The other color modes show total return or maximum drawdown relative to the rest of the grid, so their brightest cube is the best in that grid, not necessarily better than DCA. Click a cube to load its equity path and ledger; use the capture slice to inspect hidden layers. The table provides the same settings without requiring the 3D view.

Total return measures cash plus remaining holdings against initial capital. Capture count records completed full exits, not profit or the amount of growth captured. Every displayed result is in sample. To assess sensitivity, compare neighboring settings, change the date range, and evaluate a separate later period with parameters fixed in advance. Selecting the brightest cube after seeing its full history is not evidence of future performance. The interactive cube does not select a model. The separate daily research workflow fits and saves parameters as described below; it does not deploy them to a broker.

These rules are versioned as dca-va-capture-v2; reports written before 2026-09-29 carry an earlier name for the same rules. Earlier open-ledger-v1 reports delayed reentry until the next session. Rerun older experiments to compare results under the current timing rule.

Yahoo model fitting and two-year retraining

The daily research command fits only DCA, DVA, and capture. It searches a bounded grid on older Yahoo opening-price history, keeping starting capital and trading costs fixed. Selection looks for the center of a bright region, not a lone peak. Each candidate is scored by the average training final equity of the 27 grid positions within one step of it on each axis, including itself. Positions past the edge of the grid use the nearest edge cell. The highest average wins. Its own final equity, lower drawdown, fewer trades, and numeric parameter order break ties. The model also records whether the pick lies on the edge of the grid, where the region may continue past the tested values. For comparison only, it records the single best training cell and how that cell did in the later period. It reserves a later chronological block (at least 126 sessions, normally about 20% of the observations) for evaluation without using that block to select the winner. The older training block needs at least 252 sessions. Each block starts with the same initial cash.

The saved model includes the original observations, candidate scores, selected parameters, data and code hashes, training timestamp, and next retraining date. The scheduled command reuses it until two calendar years after fitting, then refits on its next successful run. Changes to configuration or strategy/training code create a separate model series. The dashboard shows the most recently saved model; its manual cubes remain exploratory.

This is parameter calibration, not a price forecast. The speaker’s exact model family, training window, objective, and formulas were not supplied; these are explicit project choices. The evaluation is retrospective and may reuse history previously explored elsewhere. The daily replay applies saved parameters over the requested history, including training data, and does not claim a continuous prospective account across retraining. Research models never silently replace the separate paper runner’s frozen policy.

Data and accounting

Yahoo’s daily Open is read with dividend adjustment disabled. The engine uses split-normalized prices and matching normalized share units; split events are recorded but do not multiply shares again. Close, adjusted close, high, and low are not inputs to the engine for Yahoo runs. Cached snapshots have a SHA-256 digest and fetch timestamp, expire after 24 hours, and are fetched as complete ranges to avoid mixing incompatible adjustment bases.

Only completed daily bars are admitted: a session is included after its exchange-calendar close plus 15 minutes, including early closes. Earlier runs use previous completed sessions. The exchange calendar determines weekends, holidays and special closures. A requested range must contain every expected session, with no extra dates. Missing or invalid prices stop a run; a Yahoo failure never silently becomes a synthetic backtest.

Cash dividends and capital-gain distributions are credited to previous holdings on the ex-date. This is an approximation: actual payment is later, so the model makes that cash available too early. Fractional shares and immediate sale-proceeds reuse are also assumed. Taxes, cash interest, settlement restrictions, spreads beyond configured slippage, partial fills, and broker order constraints are not modeled.

Purchases pay open × (1 + slippage); sales receive open × (1 − slippage). Fees are charged per order. Spending is capped by available cash. Orders too small to cover a sale fee are skipped. The comparison buys and holds the same ETF with the same initial capital and purchase costs, accumulating distributions as cash. Neither path includes a forced final liquidation.

Drawdown and final value are measured at opens, so intraday losses are not shown. These results should not be presented as intraday risk measurements or audited performance.

Leveraged funds and the 100-year series

The lab also accepts six leveraged index funds from Yahoo: SSO and SPXL/UPRO (2x and 3x S&P 500), QLD and TQQQ (2x and 3x Nasdaq-100), and UDOW (3x Dow). They reset daily, so over longer periods they do not return a multiple of the index. Their Yahoo history starts in 2006–2010, a period when every crash recovered quickly. These funds are for research only: paper trading, model fitting and daily reports keep the unleveraged list.

For earlier decades, two further sources use Kenneth French’s daily US market data (all CRSP stocks, dividends included, from July 1926). US market 1x is that market return. Simulated 3x US market earns, each day, 3 × the market’s return above T-bills plus the T-bill rate, minus 0.95% a year in expenses, the way SPXL and UPRO are built. No such fund existed before 2006, and borrowing at the T-bill rate is optimistic before the 1980s. These series are daily closing levels, so decisions use the latest close and fill at the next close. Idle cash earns the daily 1-month T-bill rate from the same file. Yahoo runs still assume no cash interest. The file is cached for a week and updated monthly by its publisher.

Open software, external data

Project code is AGPL-3.0-or-later. The interface uses native browser APIs; the application and ledger use Python and SQLite. No cloud account, external JavaScript CDN, paid service, or Node build is required. Yahoo data is external and is not relicensed as FLOSS. yfinance’s documentation describes it as an unofficial research tool and directs users to Yahoo’s data-use terms.

The synthetic mode is a deterministic interface and accounting fixture. It is identical for every ETF selection, omits weekends rather than implementing an exchange calendar, and must never be interpreted as actual market history.

Validation and paper execution

Chronological retrospective validation, cost and execution sensitivities, synthetic stress tests, and frozen prospective plans are available from the research command. The default strategy has not demonstrated an advantage over buy and hold or simple DCA on the available sample. Future evidence remains uncollected.

The separate Alpaca paper runner tracks actual virtual-account balances and fills, commits unique order intents before submission, and stops on stale quotes, mismatches, uncertain orders or configured risk thresholds. The dashboard shows its heartbeat and alerts. Credentials, a dedicated paper account and explicit runner startup are required. No live endpoint is supported. UI controls change only historical research.

What is automated today

The daily command refreshes Yahoo data, fits or reuses the saved research model, and writes a dated, idempotent research report with a next-session paper plan. From 2026-09-24 to 2026-09-29 the home server ran it in a rootless Podman container every weekday at 18:00 New York time. The server is now powered off while the project is paused. An optional systemd timer does the same on a computer without containers. This is a replayed simulation, not a persistent brokerage account. No real orders are sent.
